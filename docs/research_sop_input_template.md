# Professional Research SOP — Future Input Template

This document defines the information needed to replace the temporary generic
baseline with a versioned professional research methodology. It does not claim
any third-party proprietary methodology.

## 1. Project intake and decision definition

- questions asked when receiving a new engagement;
- how the final management decision is worded;
- mandatory client information;
- conditions that require returning the brief for clarification;
- examples of acceptable and unacceptable research objectives.

## 2. Market-definition rules

- product and service boundary;
- customer and application boundary;
- geography boundary;
- value-chain boundary;
- time boundary;
- inclusion and exclusion logic;
- treatment of adjacent, substitute, and emerging markets;
- market-definition approval role.

## 3. Research-question decomposition

- standard workstreams;
- sequencing and dependencies;
- hypothesis construction;
- information-needs mapping;
- when to split or combine tasks;
- task-level deliverable standards.

## 4. Sources and evidence

- source hierarchy;
- source-quality scoring;
- minimum corroboration requirements;
- handling of reports, expert interviews, company materials, and channel checks;
- evidence freshness rules;
- conflicting-source resolution;
- citation and evidence-matrix requirements.

## 5. Analysis methods

- market sizing and forecasting methods;
- competitor-selection and comparable-company logic;
- market-driver analysis;
- value-chain and business-model analysis;
- scenario and trend-forecast rules;
- company scorecard dimensions;
- confidence and uncertainty standards.

## 6. Review and rejection gates

- analyst self-check;
- manager review;
- expert review;
- client confirmation;
- conditions requiring more research;
- conditions that block a conclusion;
- responsibility for approving high-impact judgments.

## 7. Action Plan logic

- how findings become strategic options;
- ranking and prioritization rules;
- owner, timing, resource, KPI, risk, and stop-condition requirements;
- examples of strong and weak recommendations.

## 8. Pack metadata

- SOP display name;
- version and effective date;
- owner and reviewer;
- applicable project types;
- mandatory rules versus advisory guidance;
- permitted exceptions and exception approver.

Each supplied rule will receive a stable rule ID, for example `SUL-BRIEF-001`,
and every generated artifact will record the exact SOP version, content hash,
applicable rule IDs, and compliance checks.
